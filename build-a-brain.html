A single neuron multiplies each input by a weight, adds a bias, and fires if the result is positive. Geometrically that is one straight line. It works great when your two classes fall on opposite sides of some line, like Level 1. But real data is rarely that clean. Level 2's data forms a wedge that no single neuron can isolate, so it takes two working together. Level 3 goes further: its data surrounds a small cluster from every direction, and it turns out even two neurons cannot fully wrap it, no matter how they are angled. Only a third neuron closes the gap. This is exactly why deep learning stacks neurons into layers: each additional neuron adds another straight cut, and combining more of them lets the network carve out shapes that fewer neurons never could.
