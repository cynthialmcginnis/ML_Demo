What just happened

With one feature, training meant searching two numbers: slope and intercept, and the model was a line. With two features, it's three numbers, and the model is a plane — a flat surface tilting through square footage, age, and price all at once. Every extra feature adds one more axis to the space the model lives in and one more weight to search for. The loss function never changes: still Sum of Squared Error, still the vertical gap between where a house actually is and where the surface says it should be.

This is also the bridge to deep learning. A single neuron in a neural network computes exactly this: a weighted sum of its inputs plus a bias — the same plane-fitting math, just one small piece of a much larger network. What makes a network "deep" isn't different math, it's thousands of these weighted sums layered together, each trained the same way you just trained three numbers here.

Feature vector
All of a house's inputs together — here, [square footage, age].
Weight
How much each feature counts toward the prediction — w₁ and w₂ above.
Weighted sum
Multiply each feature by its weight, add the bias — that's the entire prediction, and it's what defines the plane's tilt.
Bias
A baseline value added regardless of the features — the intercept, b, which sets the plane's height at the origin.
Training
Searching for the weights and bias that make total error, across every house, as small as possible.
