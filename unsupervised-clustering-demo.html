Finding Structure Without Labels

The regression demos all had an answer key: a real price to predict, and a loss that measured how wrong you were against it. This data has no answer key at all — just customers, described by how much they spend and how often they visit, with no "correct" grouping attached. Unsupervised learning doesn't predict a known value. It searches for structure that was already there, waiting to be noticed.

What just happened

There was never a "right" grouping to check against — that's the whole difference from the earlier demos. Instead, the algorithm you just ran, called k-means, minimizes a loss of its own invention: how far each point sits from the center of its own group, squared and summed, exactly the same shape of loss function as Sum of Squared Error, just without a known target to compare to.

Each round did two things in turn: every customer joined whichever centroid was nearest (assignment), then each centroid moved to the average position of its members (update). Repeat that enough times and the centroids stop moving — that's convergence. Try "Randomize Centroids" and running again: you may land on a different grouping than last time. Unlike the regression demos, k-means has no guaranteed single best answer. It only guarantees it will stop somewhere it can't improve from — which might be a different somewhere depending on where it started. That's a genuine, well-documented limitation of the algorithm, not a bug in this demo.

Unlabeled data
Examples with no known answer attached — just customers and their features.
Cluster
A group of points the algorithm decided belong together, based on distance alone.
Centroid
The center point of a cluster — the average position of every member.
Inertia / WCSS
The loss being minimized: total squared distance from each point to its own cluster's center.
Convergence
The point where reassigning and recentering stop changing anything.
Local optimum
A good stopping point the algorithm found — not necessarily the best one that exists.
