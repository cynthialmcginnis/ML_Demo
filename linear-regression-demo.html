Finding the Line That Fits

A supervised learning model doesn't guess. It searches for the line, curve, or boundary that makes the smallest possible error against examples it's already seen. This demo makes that search visible — you'll do it by hand, then watch the computer do it instantly.

  1. Step 1Drag the two sliders to draw your own best guess at a line through the data.
  2. Step 2Watch the error number (SSE) as you adjust — lower is better.
  3. Step 3Click "Reveal Best-Fit Line" to see the mathematically optimal answer and how close you got.

What just happened

You picked a slope and an intercept, and the demo measured how wrong that line was for every data point, then squared each miss and added them up. That total is the loss — a single number that says how bad your model is. Smaller is better.

When you clicked "Reveal," the demo didn't guess. It solved for the exact slope and intercept that make that loss as small as it can possibly be, given this data. A real training algorithm does the same search, just automatically and thousands of times per second — nudging the line a little, checking if the loss went down, and repeating until it can't improve anymore. That process is called gradient descent, and it's how most machine learning models "learn."
